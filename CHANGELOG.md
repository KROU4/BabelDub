## [0.3.0] - 2026-10-26

Delivery tags: VoxCPM2 voices every line from the character's long original reference plus a short instruction of how this particular line is delivered, measured on the original and corrected by the translator agent. Chosen in blind listening on the 38-line test set of Low-Budget Repairs over v0.2's target-language prompt bank, although its metrics were slightly lower than the same chain without tags (CER with numbers spelled out 0.030 vs 0.020, UTMOS 2.73 vs 2.81, single raw takes).

### Added

- **Delivery tags measured on the originals** (`analysis/prosody_tags.py`). A vocabulary of 12 tags — `shouting`, `whispered`, `urgent`, `energetic`, `calm`, `sad`, `soft`, `loud`, `quick`, `slow`, `expressive`, `flat`, at most 3 per line — each an English phrase of the VoxCPM2 control text. A line's pitch, loudness and voiced share, and on lines with ≥ 1.5 s of speech its pitch range and syllable rate, become robust leave-one-out z-scores against the same character's other lines (at least 5 comparable lines); only strong deviations become tags, and a moderate pace reads "slightly faster/slower". Computed when needed from `inventory/analysis.jsonl` and the cast, never stored.
- **The translator agent corrects the delivery.** `batch make` gives every line its measured `delivery_hint` and the batch header the vocabulary (`delivery_tags`); the translator returns `delivery` only where the scene calls for another delivery (sarcasm read calm, crying, a whisper in a loud scene; `[]` = explicitly neutral, no field or `null` = accept the hint, the hint repeated = the hint; a `shorten`/`retranslate` line shows `current_delivery` and keeps it when `delivery` is left out). `batch submit` validates it (code 2 with a `hint` listing the vocabulary) and stores it on the translation row (`delivery` in `translations.jsonl`).
- **`tts.ref_strategy=tags`**: the character's long original reference (`tts.tags_reference`: `main`, the default, or `bucket`, the reference of the line's nearest arousal bucket) plus the control text `(phrases)` before the dub text; every line is voiced in one run, no waiting lines. Precedence: the translator's `delivery` > the measured tags > none; `tts.delivery_tags=on|measured|off`. Results rows carry `control`, `tags`, `tags_source` (`agent | measured | none | dropped`) and `delivery_tags`; `synth` and its dry run report `delivery{by_source, tags}` over every planned take, `status` reports `counts.delivery` over each line's latest ok take. Engines without a control text (Qwen3-TTS, Chatterbox) ignore the tags, without measuring them, with a `control_ignored` note.
- The VoxCPM2 worker takes `control`: it sends `(control)text`, counts only the dub text for its length cap and bad-case check, and drops a continuation prompt sent together with a control text (`prompt_dropped`). The `dummy` test engine accepts `control` the same way.

### Changed

- **The default VoxCPM2 chain is the long original reference + delivery tags** (`tts.ref_strategy=auto` → `tags` for `voxcpm` and `dummy`). `bootstrap` (v0.2's target-language prompt bank) stays available as an explicit setting, for example against a target-language accent. An existing project keeps its voiced takes; new and waiting lines are voiced with `tags` unless `tts.ref_strategy=bootstrap` is set.
- **Regens and delivery.** A QA `regen` after a `cer` failure, and any regen from the 3rd take of the same text on, is voiced without the control text (`tags_source: dropped`): the words matter more than the delivery. After `flat`/`mos` the tags stay with a new seed (with `tts.tags_reference=bucket` also the next nearest bucket); a `reref` keeps them. A changed control text (a new `delivery`, a re-measured line, another `tts.delivery_tags`) voices the same text again as `reason: delivery`, counted toward `tts.max_attempts`, except a take QA accepted, which a re-measured line alone leaves as it is (only a new `delivery` or another `tts.delivery_tags` voices it again); takes made before v0.3 stay done (`synth --force` re-voices them).
- A `short_word` line carries its delivery tags too; its one regen still goes through the character's long reference.
- `babeldub set` validates `tts.ref_strategy` (`auto|tags|bootstrap|line|char`), `tts.tags_reference` (`main|bucket`) and `tts.delivery_tags` (`on|measured|off`); new projects get both new settings.
- Skills, roles and agents describe the new chain: the translator checks `delivery_hint` against the scene and returns `delivery` only when it disagrees; voice, QA, cast and localize cover the settings, the regen rules and when to use `measured`, `off` or `bootstrap`. The delivery is decided by the measurement and the agents, never asked from the user: still exactly two questions. The README and its voice illustration describe the delivery tags.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-22

The dubbing pipeline v0.2: analysis of the originals, line classes, arousal-matched prompts, scored best-of, speech never slowed down, wider QA. Measured against v0.1 on a 38-line test set of Low-Budget Repairs (13 characters, the same Russian texts and metrics): slowed lines 17 → 0, time-stretched 74 % → 15 %, UTMOS 2.64 → 3.14 (originals 3.14), speaker similarity 0.544 → 0.606, the pitch-range gap to the original 5.6 → 3.4 semitones, CER of the dubbed lines 0.054 → 0.034; 5 of 38 lines keep the original.

### Added

- **Stage `analyze`** (between `link` and `cast`, qc worker): Silero VAD speech segments, the ASR of the original with word times and confidences, prosody (F0 median and range, RMS, voiced ratio, syllable rate), an opt-in emotion2vec+ label, and a line class `speech | short_word | interjection | nonverbal | mixed` → `inventory/analysis.jsonl`; the `subtitle_mismatch` flag marks subtitles the original does not say.
- **Line classes drive the dub.** Interjections and nonverbal lines keep the original: `translate` leaves them out (`translate plan` → `keep_original`), `synth` skips them (`skipped.keep_original`) with one automatic `keep_original` decision (`tts.keep_original_classes`; an agent/human decision overrides it). A `short_word` line ("Freeze!") is voiced from the line itself (no target-language prompt) with `tts.short_word_best_of` = 6 candidates and one regen at most (`tts.short_word_attempts` = 2). A passing `mixed` line is accepted with attention.
- **Arousal-matched voice prompts.** Each character's lines fall into calm / animated / loud / soft buckets by their prosody relative to the character (`cast/refs.py`, `bucket` in `refs.jsonl` and `synth/results.jsonl`). VoxCPM2 gets a long timbre reference per bucket (≥ 8 s of material, else calm) and a target-language **prompt bank**: per bucket 1–2 accepted dubs of 5–10 s ranked by MOS and prosody similarity to their original (CER only a gate), instead of one frozen prompt of the lowest-CER (and flattest) dubs. Each line takes the nearest bucket; a regen after a `flat`/`mos` failure takes the next one. A v0.1 `tgt.json` reads as the calm prompt.
- **Scored best-of** (`tts.best_of_mode=score`, the default when the `qc` env exists; `synth score`): `synth` keeps every candidate, the TTS worker exits, and the qc worker scores them (CER and length gates; 0.35 prosody/emotion similarity + 0.25 MOS + 0.2 length + 0.2 voice similarity, 0.05 for characters with an RVC model); the winner gets a new results row (`key` = sha(key, seed)) and the rest is deleted.
- **QA:** UTMOS22 naturalness against the median of the character's own originals (`mos`, `orig_mos`, `qa/mos_baseline.json`; reason `mos` below the median − `qa.mos_drop` 1.0), prosody similarity to the original (F0 range ratio and contour correlation, `prosody_sim`; reason `flat` below `qa.min_f0_range_ratio` 0.5 of the original's range), onset/offset deltas of `fixed_length` lines (`post` `offset_deltas`; reason `timing` beyond ±80 ms), and CER with digits spelled out on both sides. `timing` regens, then keeps the original; `mos` and `flat` regen, then accept the last take with attention (delivery, not words or voice).
- **Pronunciation and stress.** The glossary `pronounce` is applied to the dub text (stress-only entries reach inflected forms too). Engines declare `stress: none | upper | acute` in `models.yaml` (VoxCPM2 `upper`: a CAPITAL stressed vowel, since it reads `+` as "plus"; the ru-stress model `acute`). `tts.stress=on` (off by default: plain text was best in the A/B) marks Russian homographs with RUAccent through the qc worker's new `accent` op (cached in `synth/accent.jsonl`). The qc worker also gained `spell` (num2words) and an F0 contour in `prosody`/`score`.
- **The default dubbing chain is VoxCPM2 + bootstrap prompts + best of 3 + exact fit + RVC** (chosen in blind listening rounds on Low-Budget Repairs, English → Russian: CER 0.045 vs 0.050 for Qwen3-TTS + RVC, ECAPA ~0.65–0.70 to the originals, which score 0.77 among themselves).
  - Engine `voxcpm` (VoxCPM2, Apache-2.0, 2B, 48 kHz, 30 languages; worker `tts_voxcpm.py`; peak VRAM 6.5 GB). `tts.engine=auto` (the new default written by `init`) runs it on CUDA when its env exists, else `qwen3tts-fast`/`qwen3tts`/`chatterbox`, with the choice in `notes`; CUDA-only engines fall back along their chain.
  - `tts.ref_strategy=bootstrap` (what `auto` means for VoxCPM2): a ~15–20 s long timbre reference of 3–4 clean original lines for every line; the first 4 lines of each character with it alone, the rest after QA accepted 3 of them, with a frozen target-language prompt built from those dubs and their exact text (`synth/prompts/`). Waiting lines are reported by `synth`, `qa` and `status`.
  - Best of N (`tts.best_of=3`, `synth --best-of`): N takes with different seeds; without the `qc` env the one closest to the original's speech length (energy VAD, −42 dB) is kept, with it they are scored (above).
  - Stage `vc` and `vc train`: one RVC v2 model per main character (Applio, MIT; trained on its clean original lines, 20 epochs, up to 30 minutes), applied after `post` with the exact length and loudness re-applied; characters without a model pass through. `setup` installs `voxcpm` and the git engine `applio` (pinned commit, requirements without torch, prerequisites) on GPUs with 8 GB+.
- **Voice clusters merge into named characters.** After clustering, `cast` compares every cluster's centroid with the voice centroid of each character from metadata (up to 12 longest clean originals); a cluster at or above `cast.merge_threshold` (default 0.75) is that character: its lines are assigned to him (`conf` = the centroid cosine), `characters.jsonl` records `merged_clusters`, the casting sheet lists the merges, and the output has `merged` and `merged_into`. Clusters are not merged with each other beyond the clustering itself. On Low-Budget Repairs the 50 clusters of lines without speaker metadata included `cluster_04` (0.88 to Zbyszek) and `cluster_05` (0.89 to Maciek), their cutscene lines; every other pair scored 0.06–0.38.
- **QA speaker identity margin.** When at least two characters have voice centroids (the checked characters plus every selected or main one), `qa` records `margin` (similarity to the own character's centroid minus the best other character's) and `closest_char`, and fails a dub with the reason `identity` below `qa.min_identity_margin` (default −0.05; auto-decision `regen`, then `keep_original`). Characters whose voices are at least `cast.merge_threshold` similar count as one voice.

### Changed

- **Evaluation on the 38-line test set (lab v0.2) tuned the defaults:** QA accepts with attention a last take that fails only `mos`/`flat` instead of keeping the original (`qa.decisions.SOFT_REASONS`); a `short_word` regen uses the character's own reference instead of the line; a `fixed_length` line that would end early gets longer pauses (`pause_add_s`, never slower speech), `tts.fixed_length_best_of` = 5 candidates and a heavier length weight in the scored best-of.

- **`post` fits every dub exactly and never slows speech down:** a dub that does not fit first loses part of its pauses, then speeds up to `post.tempo_max` (1.15, pitch kept; `post.tempo_min` 1.0); `fixed_length` lines are placed chunk by chunk on the original's speech segments; the rest is silence, the speech at the original's speech start, exactly the original's sample count, rate and channels, loudness of the original speech; a dub that does not fit is never cut (`not_exact[]`). `post.max_stretch` is gone.
- **`translation.mode=auto` resolves to `adapt`** when the game has official target-language texts: the translator turns them into spoken dub text (numbers as words, Latin names transliterated, colloquial, length budget) and the official text stays the subtitle; `official` is chosen only explicitly.
- **U+0301 stress marks are stripped** from dub texts for every model except an explicit stress model: VoxCPM2 and Qwen3-TTS Base voice them as extra sounds (with `tts.stress=on`, VoxCPM2 gets a capital vowel instead).
- **The translation budget** is the original's speech span times the character's measured speaking rate (median over its accepted dubs), and the original's pauses of 250 ms or more are shown to the translator as ` / `.
- QA checks the final (converted) take; the build packs the latest post row's audio and lists the RVC models in the manifest.

- **QA similarity is calibrated for cross-language dubs.** A dub in another language than the original audio (`source_lang` ≠ `target_lang`, the normal case) is judged with `qa.min_sim_ratio_xlang` = 0.45 × `sim_ref`; same-language dubs keep `qa.min_sim_ratio` = 0.75; an absolute floor `qa.min_sim_abs` = 0.25 applies to both. `qa.jsonl` records `sim_ratio` and `sim_threshold`. On Low-Budget Repairs (Russian Qwen3-TTS dub of English originals) the old bar 0.75 × 0.76 = 0.57 failed 27 of 30 dubs whose Whisper CER median was 0.0; good dubs scored 0.38–0.66 against the hero's centroid, clear failures 0.08–0.11.
- **Nonverbal lines skip the voice and CER checks.** Screams and laughs (`nonverbal` flag) are measured but never fail on `sim`, `identity` or `cer` (`sim_skipped`, `cer_skipped` in `qa.jsonl`).
- `babeldub set` knows the new settings (`cast.merge_threshold`, `qa.min_sim_ratio_xlang`, `qa.min_sim_abs`, `qa.min_identity_margin`, `tts.best_of_mode`, `tts.stress`, `qa.mos_drop`, `qa.min_f0_range_ratio`), and new projects get them in `project.json`.

### Fixed

- `babeldub install`/`reapply`: the names of the game's executables reach PowerShell through the environment, never as script text (a crafted file name with a typographic quote could run commands).
- QA re-checks a line whose winner `synth score` swapped after the check (`src_key`, `post_ts` in `qa.jsonl`), and the bootstrap prompt bank takes only current QA rows; `synth score` writes each line's results row right after its winner, so a crash leaves finished lines consistent.
- `post` re-places a `fixed_length` line after `analyze` changed its speech segments (`vad_key`).
- The translation budget uses the speech span (first to last sound, pauses included), matching how the speaking rate is measured.
- Projects created by 0.1 get the new tempo defaults once (`post.tempo_min` 0.93 → 1.0, `tempo_max` 1.2 → 1.15; `migrations` in `project.json`).
- `analyze --limit` no longer marks the stage done while stale lines remain; `link` points to `analyze`, and `cast` warns when it was skipped.
- A "dub anyway" decision on an interjection also reaches `translate`.
- `vc train` keeps only the latest RVC checkpoint and rounds the epoch count to a multiple of 10.
- An empty MOS baseline is not cached; stale emotion vectors are dropped on re-analysis.

### Security

- Workflows run with read-only tokens except the release job; actions are pinned by commit SHA, the Claude Code CLI by version.
- Engine packages are pinned to exact versions, git engines to commits, Hugging Face models to revisions; the UTMOS22 checkpoint loads with `weights_only=True`.
- The game-folder guard resolves Windows 8.3 short names and symlinks before comparing paths (it remains a heuristic, not a sandbox).

### Not in this release

- Emotion similarity and DNSMOS in QA: emotion2vec+ is opt-in (non-commercial license), so delivery is judged by prosody (F0 range and contour) and UTMOS22; arousal buckets come from prosody, not from emotion vectors.
- Separation and EQ matching for radio/phone lines (plan step 6).

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-21

First public release: the full pipeline for localizing and dubbing a game in the voices of its original characters, driven by an AI agent.

### Added

- **Dubbing in the original character's voice.** The voice prompt for each line is the original line itself with its source text (`tts.ref_strategy=line`), so the timbre, emotion and delivery of that exact phrase carry over. Short, noisy and untranscribed lines use the character's best reference (`char`); by default the strategy is chosen automatically (`auto`).
- **Skills following the Agent Skills standard:** `babeldub-localize` (orchestrator), `babeldub-doctor`, `babeldub-recon`, `babeldub-cast`, `babeldub-translate`, `babeldub-voice`, `babeldub-qa`, `babeldub-pack`. The same files work in Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Antigravity, Cursor, Copilot, OpenCode and when uploaded to claude.ai.
- **Claude Code subagents** for the pipeline roles (recon, casting, glossary, translation, voice, QA, packing). They are generated from `skills/babeldub-localize/references/roles/`; on other hosts the same roles run in the main session.
- **Claude Code hooks:** protection of a registered game folder against writes that bypass `babeldub install`, and a summary of the active project at session start.
- **The `babeldub` CLI** with one contract (one JSON object on output, exit codes 0–4): environment (`doctor`, `setup`, `models`), project (`init`, `status`, `set`, `query`, `sample`, `games`), game analysis (`scan`, `extract`, `link`), characters (`cast`), translation (`translate`, `batch`, `glossary`), voice (`synth`, `post`), quality control (`qa`, `decide`, `report`), build and install (`build`, `verify`, `install`, `uninstall`, `reapply`).
- **GPU-first compute:** when an NVIDIA GPU is present, all neural compute (TTS, ASR, speaker embeddings, clustering) runs on it (`compute.device=auto`), and `setup` installs CUDA builds of torch whenever a GPU is visible.
- **Format adapters:** FMOD Studio (`.bank`/FSB5), Unity Localization tables, Wwise, loose audio files. `babeldub scan` shows the support level (A/B/C) for a particular game.
- **`qwen3tts-fast`, the default TTS engine on NVIDIA GPUs.** The same Qwen3-TTS models through `faster-qwen3-tts` 0.5.4 (CUDA graphs, own venv with `transformers==5.15.1`; 5.20.x breaks the codec config). On an RTX 3060 Ti with 1.7B-Base: RTF ≈ 0.52 per line versus ≈ 3.7 (single line) and ≈ 1.2–1.4 (batches of 4) for the official `qwen-tts`, peak VRAM ≈ 4.5 GB, identical audio for the same seed and the same Whisper CER. `setup` installs it whenever a GPU is visible and never on the `cpu` tier; `synth` falls back to the official `qwen3tts` (and says so in `notes`) on the CPU or without its env; `tts.engine=qwen3tts` forces the official package. Takes of either count as done for the other.
- **Alternative takes and duplicates are dubbed once.** Lines of one container that share a subtitle key and are flagged `alt_take`/`duplicate` follow a leader (the take named like the key, else an unflagged one, else `_BEST`, else the first): only the leader is synthesized, post-processed and checked, and the build gives every follower the leader's accepted dub (`via` in the build manifest). Alt takes often do not match their subtitle, and on Low-Budget Repairs an ICL prompt with such a take looped for 55.9 s on a 3.8 s line.
- **Runaway guard for voice cloning.** A per-line generation cap from the estimated dub length and the original (`max_new_tokens = ceil(12.5 · max(6, 2 · max(est_s, target_s) + 2.5))`); a take that hits the cap or runs far past a plausible length is discarded and the line is retried alone, timbre-only on the same prompt and then with the character reference, so one loop no longer drags a whole batch to the cap. An ICL pre-check sends lines whose transcript cannot fit their audio (outside 1.2–8.5 syllables per second) to the character reference or timbre-only mode. Batches group lines of similar estimated length. `synth/results.jsonl` records `ref_mode` (`line|line-xvec|char|char-xvec`), `ref_reason`, `retry_reason` and the `discarded` takes.
- **Quality control:** back-transcription of the dub (CER against the translation; faster-whisper with a voice-activity filter and without conditioning on previous text, so silent or noisy tails do not produce hallucinated text), similarity of the synthesized voice to the character's original, duration and peaks; automatic `regen`/`reref`/`shorten` decisions by thresholds.
- **Install with rollback:** backup of the originals with sha1 checks, atomic replacement, `uninstall`, and `reapply` after a game update.
- **Data location:** environments, models, tools and projects live in `BABELDUB_HOME`, by default `~/.babeldub` (`C:\Users\<user>\.babeldub` on Windows); `doctor` reports data left in older default locations.
- **Packaging for hosts:** Claude Code marketplace and plugin, `.codex-plugin`, Agent Plugins 1.0 (`plugin.json`), Gemini CLI extension (`gemini-extension.json`, `GEMINI.md`).
- **Releases:** `babeldub-plugin`, `babeldub-skills` (one zip per skill for claude.ai) and `babeldub-kit` (a workspace folder next to the game), `SHA256SUMS`.
- **Helper scripts:** `bump_version.py`, `validate_skills.py`, `build_release.py`, `install.ps1`, `install.sh`; CI on Ubuntu and Windows.
- **Documentation (English):** install per host (`docs/HOSTS.md`), engine support (`docs/ENGINES.md`), models and hardware tiers (`docs/MODELS.md`), responsible use (`docs/LEGAL.md`), the CLI contract (`docs/dev/CONTRACT.md`); a Russian README (`README.ru.md`).
